1. Who we are and how to reach us

Qwilted is an iPhone app that saves the links, videos and posts you share from other apps, lets you find any of them again by asking, and arranges them on a board you can share. Stitch is the character in the app who answers your questions about your saves. Your board is called your Qwilt, and its patches are your collections.

Qwilted is made and run by Jacob Hanks, an individual. It isn't a company. In this policy, "Qwilted", "we", "us" and "our" mean Jacob Hanks, and "you" means anyone who uses the app.

  • Privacy questions and requests: privacy@qwilted.app
  • Everything else: support@qwilted.app

Jacob Hanks is the person responsible for protecting personal information at Qwilted, including under Canadian law. We don't have a public postal address. Email is the way to reach us, and we answer every privacy email.

This policy covers the Qwilted app (including the part of it that appears in other apps' Share menus), the Qwilted server the app talks to, and this website. It doesn't cover other apps or websites, including the sites behind the links you save.

2. The short version

  • Your library lives on your phone. Your saves, notes, patches, your Qwilt, your to-dos and what Stitch remembers about you are stored on your phone. There's no sign-up, and our server keeps no copy of your library.
  • Cloud AI is your choice. If you choose Cloud AI, the save being read or the question you ask goes to our server, which passes it to Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, to write a note or an answer. Our server doesn't keep it, and Anthropic doesn't train on it. If you choose Phone only, none of it is sent. You can change your mind in Settings at any time.
  • Two sharing switches, both off unless you turn them on. "Share how I use Qwilted" sends daily counts, never what you saved. "Help improve Stitch" sends your questions, his answers and your thumbs up or down, which we keep for 12 months to fix wrong answers.
  • Some things happen whatever you choose. To show a link's title and picture, your phone contacts the site the link came from. And while Qwilted is open, it checks now and then that it can reach our server, even with Phone only, so like any internet service our server sees your phone's network (IP) address.
  • No ads, no tracking, no selling. There are no ads, no advertising ID, no tracking across other apps or websites, and no third-party analytics code in the app. We never sell your information.
  • Qwilted is for adults. You must be 18 or older to use it.

3. What we collect

"Collect" here means any information about you that the app stores, or that leaves your phone. It's split by where it goes.

3.1 Kept on your phone only

These stay in Qwilted's storage on your phone. We can't see them.

  • Your library. Your saves (links, titles, captions, the app each came from and when you saved it), notes, patches, your Qwilt (where patches sit, the threads you tie between them and the labels you write on it), and your to-dos with their deadlines and reminders. A to-do you make from a save remembers which save it came from and that save's title.
  • Words Qwilted keeps so you can find things. The text of a saved web page, words spoken in a video, and text read from screenshots and video frames. Qwilted reads these on your phone.
  • Pictures. Photos, screenshots and videos you add, which you pick with Apple's photo picker (it gives Qwilted only the ones you pick). Qwilted also keeps a copy of each save's preview picture, because many sites' picture links stop working after a few days. Each copy is at most 1,200 pixels across, all of them together at most 256 MB, and a copy is deleted when its save is deleted for good.
  • Work done on your phone. Transcribing videos you add, reading text in pictures, finding saves, and some notes and answers run on your phone with Apple's built-in tools, including Apple's on-device AI when your phone has it. That work stays on your phone.
  • What Stitch remembers. The nickname you give him and what you tell him during setup, such as how you like answers. It's kept only on your phone. One part of it leaves your phone: with Cloud AI on, if you told him whether you like short answers or more detail, that choice goes with your questions about your saves as one number, about how many words to aim for, so Claude answers the way you asked (3.2). Everything else he remembers is never sent.
  • How you file. When you move a save to a different patch, Qwilted notes it on your phone so it can file the next similar save the same way. With Cloud AI on, a short summary of it goes with each save that's read (3.2).
  • Your question history. The questions you ask Stitch, how each was answered and how long it took, up to the newest 5,000. When you give an answer a thumbs down, or correct Stitch on your next question, Qwilted also notes the question, the answer, the titles of the saves it showed and your correction, up to the newest 2,000, so a wrong answer can be found later. These records never leave your phone. (The "Help improve Stitch" switch in 3.3 is separate.)
  • Feedback you give in the app. When Qwilted asks how it's going and you answer, your answers (the newest 100) are kept on your phone. They are not sent to us.
  • Your editing history. So you can undo while you edit a note, Qwilted holds your recent changes in memory. They're gone when you quit the app, and they're never written to storage or sent.
  • Usage counts. Daily counts of things like saves and questions, kept for 45 days, so Settings can show how you use Qwilted. These stay on your phone. The "Share how I use Qwilted" switch (3.3) collects its own counts, and only while it's on.
  • Crash reports. If Qwilted crashes or freezes, iOS gives the app a technical report. Qwilted keeps the 10 newest on your phone and never uploads them.
  • Automatic backups. When you leave the app and your library has changed, Qwilted may save a backup of your library on your phone, at most once a day, keeping the 3 newest. They include your saves, notes and the words Qwilted kept from pages and videos, but not pictures. If your library ever can't be opened, Qwilted also keeps a rescue copy on your phone before it starts fresh.
  • Saves waiting to be added. When you share something to Qwilted from another app, it waits on your phone until Qwilted opens. The Share menu never contacts our server (see 3.6 for the one thing it may fetch from the web).
  • Your age answer. See section 10. Qwilted keeps only whether you're 18 or older, or that setup was stopped, never your age range.
  • Settings and plan. Your Cloud AI choice, the two sharing switches, notification times, whether you have Plus (with Plus, Apple's signed record of your subscription), how much Cloud AI you've used, and the date your free trial started. The trial date is also kept in the iPhone Keychain so that reinstalling can't restart the trial. (With Cloud AI on, your plan also travels with each request, as 3.2 and 3.5 explain.)
  • Reminders and notifications. Created and scheduled on your phone. We don't send notifications from a server.
  • A random install ID. A random code made for this copy of Qwilted and kept in the iPhone Keychain. It contains nothing about you, and it's sent only with Cloud AI requests (3.2). It stays in the Keychain if you delete the app. "Delete account and data" in Settings erases it (section 9.2).

3.2 Sent when you use Cloud AI

Only if you choose Cloud AI, during setup or in Settings. With Phone only, none of this is sent. Before anything goes to Anthropic, Qwilted asks you, and shows what Cloud AI sends.

With Cloud AI on, Qwilted sends information to our server at two moments. Each request carries one save or one question, never your whole library.

When a save is read. During your free trial and with Plus, each new save is sent so Stitch can write its note and suggest a patch for it. Saves you already had may also be sent: up to 25 a day during the trial, or one at a time when you ask Qwilted to read a save. A screenshot or picture shared on its own, with no link, is read too, during your free trial and with Plus, if your phone found text in it: only that text is sent (up to 16,000 characters), never the picture. A picture with no text in it isn't sent. Qwilted sends:

  • the full link (the whole web address), when the save has one;
  • the save's title and caption, and the app it came from;
  • text from the web page, up to 16,000 characters during your free trial and with Plus and up to 6,000 otherwise, and how long the whole page was (a number);
  • during your free trial and with Plus, words spoken in a video (up to 16,000 characters) and text read from its frames (up to 500 characters);
  • text read from a screenshot or picture, whether it was shared with a link or on its own;
  • your note on it: the note you typed when you saved it, and a note you wrote or changed later, each once;
  • the names of all your patches, only when Qwilted is choosing where to file the save;
  • a short hint about how you usually file things (up to 280 characters), which can name up to 2 of your patches and the kinds of saves you put in them. It goes with every save that's read.

When you ask Stitch something that needs Cloud AI. Qwilted sends:

  • your question;
  • your last 4 questions and the first 240 characters of each of Stitch's answers, so follow-up questions make sense, with the random IDs of the saves each of those answers showed you, and of any save you told Stitch wasn't the one you meant;
  • excerpts from saves that match your question, including notes and to-dos you wrote. For each save: its title, a random ID for it, the app it came from, the day you saved it, what kind of save it is (such as "YouTube video" or "to-do"), its tags, its patches, who made it (such as a creator's name and handle, as the app you shared from or the link named them) and the website its link is on (the site's name only, never the rest of the address), each of those two at most 100 characters; and up to 2,000 characters of its words: your own note first, including the note you typed when you saved it, then its spoken words, the page's note, page text and on-screen text. Sometimes also a short passage (about 300 characters) of a video's spoken words that matches your question;
  • for a to-do, its deadline (the day of the week, the date and the time), whether it's overdue and whether it's done;
  • if you told Stitch during setup whether you like short answers or more detail, that choice as one number: about how many words to aim for (3.1);
  • how much: when Stitch looks through your saves step by step, the titles, excerpts, creators and sites for one question add up to at most 16,000 characters, including a short list of up to 20 likely saves at 160 characters each. When Stitch answers in one go, he sends at most 8 saves, or at most 20 when he's putting something together for you, such as a list or a plan;
  • counts about your library when your question needs them, such as how many saves you have by app, type and topic, and the dates of your first and latest saves;
  • your phone's current date and time with its offset from UTC, and which day your week starts on, so "this week" means your week, and the days your phone took any date words in your question to mean (such as "last week");
  • sometimes, a draft of Stitch's reply, which can mention save titles and counts, so Claude can make it read naturally.

If your question isn't about your saves, Stitch may answer from Claude's general knowledge. Then only your question and your recent conversation are sent. If Stitch offers a to-do, the to-do is made on your phone, and only if you choose to keep it.

A message about suicide, self-harm or someone in danger. Stitch answers it with fixed words that list crisis lines, written in advance by us, never by AI. When your phone recognises such a message, it isn't sent to Cloud AI at all. If one reaches our server, the server answers with the same fixed words before any AI is used and keeps nothing from it. If Claude is the first to recognise one, our server replaces Claude's reply with the same fixed words. A conversation like this is never kept in the "Help improve Stitch" log (3.3), and it's never sent again as part of a later question.

Attached to every Cloud AI request:

  • the random install ID (3.1), used to keep usage limits fair;
  • the app's access key, which is the same in every copy of Qwilted and says nothing about you;
  • the app's build number, and one word for the plan your phone thinks it's on ("free", "trial" or "plus");
  • if you have Plus, Apple's signed record of your subscription, so our server can check with Apple that it's paid for (3.5);
  • if you signed in with Apple in an early test version of Qwilted, the anonymous Apple user ID that sign-in created (never your name or email). Current versions don't offer sign-in.

Never sent: your whole library, your nickname or what you told Stitch about yourself (except whether you like short answers or more detail), your photos, videos or audio, the pictures Qwilted keeps, your crash reports, your backups, your feedback answers, or your age.

3.3 Sent only if you turn on a sharing switch

There are two switches, offered during setup and in Settings. Both are off unless you turn them on, and you can turn either off at any time. Turning a switch off stops sending straight away and throws away anything still waiting to be sent. It doesn't delete what was already sent (section 9.2 says how to delete that).

"Share how I use Qwilted." Qwilted sends a day's counts once the day is over, and today's counts so far when you leave the app, for any day in the last 45 not yet sent. The counts are:

  • how often you open Qwilted and how many days it's been since you installed it, which tabs you use, how far you got in setup and how long each setup screen took, and how many times setup stopped at the age question (never your age);
  • how many things you saved, from which apps and of which kinds (like "TikTok · video"), how you saved them, and whether they arrived;
  • how many questions you asked Stitch, whether each was answered with Cloud AI or on your phone, what kind of question it was, how many found nothing, and how many got a thumbs up or down or a score from 1 to 5;
  • how often you moved, resized, recoloured, hid, tied or labelled things on your Qwilt, changed its background or shared a card, and how many to-dos you or Stitch made and how many were finished;
  • how many patches you have, how many saves are unsorted, and whether your patch names are common words from a fixed list, such as "Recipes" or "Travel" (any other name is counted only as "custom");
  • how many free trials started and ended, Plus purchases and which plan was bought, how often Plus lapsed, times the Plus screen was shown or closed, and how often Cloud AI stepped down to your phone and why;
  • how often Stitch's notifications were shown and opened;
  • how many times the app crashed and in which build (never the crash report), and how long some things took, such as saving (as a middle value and a 90th-percentile value);
  • how often the feedback question was shown or answered (never your answers);
  • the day, the app version and the analytics ID (below).

It never includes titles, links, notes, questions or anything you wrote.

"Help improve Stitch." When a turn of your conversation with Stitch ends, Qwilted sends:

  • your question exactly as you typed it (up to 2,000 characters);
  • Stitch's answer as it read at the end (up to 6,000 characters), which can name or quote your saves;
  • the kind of question, whether it was answered with Cloud AI or on your phone, how many saves the answer used (a number, never which ones), and your thumbs up or down if you gave one;
  • the day, the app version and the analytics ID.

We use these to find wrong answers and fix Stitch. A message about suicide, self-harm or someone in danger, and the crisis answer to it, are never sent or kept (3.2), even with this switch on. If you give that answer a thumbs up or down with "Share how I use Qwilted" on, only the number of thumbs is sent, after the day is over, with no analytics ID, and our server adds it to one total for the day.

The analytics ID. Everything the two switches send carries a random ID, made the first time you turn a switch on. It's separate from the install ID used for Cloud AI, and the two are never sent together, so they can't be joined. It's kept in Qwilted's own storage, not the Keychain, so deleting the app deletes it. Delete Account erases it too.

Where it's kept. In Qwilted's database, hosted by Render in Oregon, USA, for 12 months, then deleted automatically. Each day's counts (never questions or answers) are also written to our server's logs, which Render keeps for up to 30 days.

3.4 Technical information our server receives

Whenever the app contacts our server:

  • Your IP address, which is your phone's network address, with the time, the address requested and the result. Our server uses it to limit how many requests one network can send, and it's written in the server's logs, which Render keeps for up to 30 days. We don't use it to work out where you are.
  • Basic connection details your phone sends with any web request, such as the app's name and version and the iOS version.
  • The connection check. While Qwilted is open on your screen, it checks that it can reach our server: when it opens, about once a minute after that, and when your network changes. This starts the first time you open the app, during setup, and happens even with Phone only. It sends the app's access key (and a leftover Apple user ID, if one is stored from an early test version), and nothing you wrote, saved or answered.
  • Usage-limit counters. To keep Cloud AI fair and affordable, our server counts requests and spending per install ID, per Plus subscription and per IP address, with reading your saves and Stitch's answers counted separately. Each counter is named with a scrambled code made from the ID or address (a keyed hash), never the ID or address itself. The counters hold no content. Most expire within about 7 days, and a counter for a single question within 10 minutes. A Plus subscription's counts for the calendar month (how many Cloud AI answers it has had and what it has spent) are kept for 40 days, and so are the month's two running totals of all spending, one for Plus and one for everyone else, which have no ID.
  • Cost records. For each Cloud AI call, our server logs what it cost us: how much text was processed, the price, the kind of request, whether the server treated it as free or Plus, and the plan word your phone sent. There's no ID and nothing you wrote. We also keep daily totals of these, split by free and Plus, for 12 months.

Our server runs on Render. Every request reaches it through Cloudflare's network, which Render uses in front of its servers.

3.5 Purchases

Qwilted Plus is sold through Apple's App Store, and Apple handles payment. We never receive your card details, your name, your email address or your Apple Account.

  • Checking your plan. Your phone asks Apple whether Plus is active. With Plus and Cloud AI on, each Cloud AI request also carries Apple's signed record of your subscription: the product, the purchase and expiry dates, Apple's transaction numbers, the App Store country, the price and currency, any offer or offer code used, and codes Apple adds so the record can be checked. Our server checks Apple's signature, then asks Apple whether the subscription is still paid for. It remembers the answer in memory for up to 15 minutes, logs nothing about the purchase, and names your Plus spending counter with a scrambled code made from Apple's transaction number (3.4).
  • The free trial of Cloud AI lasts 14 days from your first save (or your first Cloud AI answer, if that comes first) and is tracked on your phone.
  • Counts only. With "Share how I use Qwilted" on, the daily counts include trials and purchases (3.3).

3.6 Things your phone fetches from other sites

When you save a link, your phone contacts other services directly, whether or not you use Cloud AI. Our server isn't involved.

  • Link previews. To show a title, a picture and who made it, your phone sends the link to the public preview services of YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, Pinterest or Spotify, or opens the web page itself.
  • Page text. For a web page, your phone reads the page to keep its words (3.1). A save that doesn't have them yet is read once, the first time you open it.
  • Pictures. Your phone loads preview pictures from those sites. Qwilted keeps a copy of each save's picture (3.1): if the picture has already been loaded to show you, it keeps that; if not, it downloads a copy only on Wi-Fi and never in Low Data Mode.
  • Page titles in the Share menu. When the app you share from doesn't include a web page's title, the Share menu reads the start of the page (at most 64 KB, for at most 3 seconds) to find it, with no cookies and no Qwilted ID.
  • Video captions. If "Transcribe videos automatically" is on (it is, unless you turn it off), your phone may ask YouTube for a video's public captions.

Those sites receive your IP address, the link and basic connection details, and they may collect more under their own privacy policies. Qwilted sends them no ID of its own. Like a web browser, your phone may keep and send back cookies that some of those sites set.

3.7 Emails and the beta sign-up form

  • Email. If you email us, we get your address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply and to keep a record of privacy requests. Email to qwilted.app goes through Cloudflare, which forwards it to our inbox at Google (Gmail).
  • The beta sign-up form. If you ask to test Qwilted before release, the form asks for your name, email, country, iPhone model and iOS version, and a few questions about how you save things. We use your answers to choose who to invite, to send your invite and beta updates, and to understand who finds Qwilted useful. We give your email to Apple only so TestFlight can send your invite. 3.8 What we don't collect

Qwilted doesn't ask for or collect:

  • your name, email address or phone number (unless you email us or sign up for the beta);
  • your contacts or calendar;
  • your location, or your photo library (only the photos you pick);
  • your camera or microphone (transcription listens to videos you add, not to you);
  • health or fitness information;
  • an advertising ID, or your activity in other apps and websites.

Your saves may contain personal things, because you choose what to save. We don't look for, sort or use sensitive information in them.

5. Cloud AI and Anthropic

  • Who does the AI work. Anthropic, PBC, which makes Claude. Our server sends Anthropic the information in 3.2. Anthropic sees our server, not your phone, so it doesn't get your IP address. Anthropic processes this for us as our service provider, under its Commercial Terms of Service and Data Processing Addendum, and not for its own purposes.
  • No training. Anthropic's Commercial Terms say Anthropic "may not train models on Customer Content from Services." We don't train AI models on your content either.
  • How long Anthropic keeps it. Anthropic deletes what we send, and what Claude writes back, within 30 days. It may keep it longer where the law requires, or up to 2 years if it's flagged under Anthropic's Usage Policy, with its safety scores for that request kept up to 7 years (Anthropic Privacy Center, "How long do you store my organization's data?", updated July 1, 2026).
  • Our server doesn't keep it. It handles each request in memory, passes it to Anthropic, returns the result and discards it. An automated test checks on every code change that request content isn't written to logs. The only content our server keeps is the "Help improve Stitch" log, which you have to turn on (3.3).
  • You'll know when Cloud AI isn't used. When an answer comes from your phone instead of Cloud AI, the answer is marked that way and Stitch tells you why: for example Phone only, no internet, the trial or allowance used up, or Cloud AI couldn't finish an answer.
  • AI can be wrong. Notes, patch suggestions and answers are written by AI and can be incomplete or wrong. Check anything important. Stitch is an AI, not a person.
  • No automated decisions about you. Qwilted doesn't make decisions based only on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects on you. Sorting a save, answering a question, or answering on your phone when a limit is reached doesn't decide anything about your rights, money, job, housing or health.

  • Nobody else has access to our server's settings, database or logs.

9. Your choices and rights

9.1 Controls in the app

  • Phone only or Cloud AI. Choose during setup and change it any time in Settings. Turning Cloud AI off stops new sending straight away. It can't recall what was already sent.
  • The two sharing switches. In Settings. Both are off unless you turn them on.
  • Transcribe videos automatically. Turn it off in Settings to stop video transcription and the YouTube caption request.
  • Notifications. Choose when Stitch may send notes during setup, or turn notifications off in iOS Settings.
  • Delete a save. It goes to Recently Deleted for 30 days, where you can restore it or erase it now.
  • Export a backup. Settings saves your whole library as one file you can keep anywhere. It's also how to get a portable copy of your data.

9.2 Delete account and data

"Delete account and data" is in Settings (we call it Delete Account below). On your phone it erases:

  • your library: saves, notes, patches, your Qwilt with its threads and labels, and your to-dos;
  • the photos, screenshots and videos you added, and the pictures Qwilted kept;
  • what Stitch remembers about you, how you file, your question history, your notes on wrong answers, and your feedback answers;
  • automatic backups, rescue copies, cached web pictures, and anything waiting in the Share menu;
  • your Cloud AI choice, the sharing switches and the analytics ID;
  • usage counts, crash reports, the install ID in the Keychain, and any leftover Apple user ID;
  • reminders and notifications waiting to be delivered.

It also asks our server to delete everything the sharing switches sent under your analytics ID (the daily counts and the question log). If your phone is offline, it keeps asking until our server confirms. Until then, the analytics ID stays on your phone inside that one request, and is used for nothing else.

What Delete Account can't reach:

  • backups you exported yourself, and iPhone backups (iCloud or computer) made before you deleted;
  • what Anthropic keeps for its limited time (section 5);
  • server logs, until they expire (up to 30 days);
  • Apple's purchase records.

What it keeps on purpose: the date your free trial started, in the Keychain, so that deleting and starting over can't restart the trial. It's one date and says nothing about what you saved.

Deleting your account doesn't cancel Plus. Apple bills it. Cancel it in iPhone Settings, under your Apple Account, then Subscriptions.

If you just delete the app, iOS removes Qwilted's storage from your phone, except the install ID and the trial date in the Keychain. Anything the sharing switches already sent stays on our side, and because the analytics ID was deleted with the app, we can no longer link it to you. It's deleted after 12 months. To have it deleted now, use Delete Account before you delete the app.

10. Age: Qwilted is for adults

Qwilted is for people 18 and older. Nobody under 18 may use it.

  • Age comes first. Setup asks about age on its second screen, before your nickname, any save or the Cloud AI choice. If your iPhone can answer, Apple shows its own screen asking whether to share your age range with Qwilted (Apple's Declared Age Range), and Apple tells Qwilted only a range, never your birthday. If you don't share, or your iPhone can't answer, setup asks you to pick a range: Under 13, 13–17, 18–24, 25–34, or 35 or older. The range is read on your phone and is never stored or sent.
  • Under 18. If Apple says you're under 18, or you pick Under 13 or 13–17, setup stops and Stitch kindly explains that Qwilted is for people 18 and up. If you picked a range by mistake, you can choose once more. Nothing you entered is saved or sent. Qwilted keeps only that setup is stopped, why, how many under-18 answers were given, and (if you picked the range yourself) the day, so the stop can lift later. It never keeps your age.
  • Nothing about you reaches our server before this. From the first screen, the app checks that it can reach our server (3.4). That check carries no answer, no name and no save, only what every internet connection shows, such as your network address.
  • Children under 13. Qwilted isn't directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them.
  • If we learn someone under 18 is using Qwilted, we'll delete anything on our side we can link to them and ask them to stop. Their library is on their phone, where we can't reach it. Delete Account, or deleting the app, removes it.
  • Parents. If you believe your child has used Qwilted, email privacy@qwilted.app.
  • Testers. People testing Qwilted before release must also be 18 or older.
  • A teen mode may come later. Before anyone under 18 is allowed to use Qwilted, we'll update this policy and tell you in the app first (section 15).

11. Security

What we do:

  • Encrypted connections. Every connection the app makes to our server uses HTTPS, and the app's settings don't allow unencrypted web connections.
  • No database of your content. Our server doesn't keep what you send with Cloud AI, and an automated test checks on every change that request content isn't written to logs.
  • Scrambled counters. Usage-limit counters are named with a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) of the ID or address, never the ID or address itself. They expire within days, and neither the counters nor our database can be reached from the public internet.
  • A locked data page. Jacob's data page needs a long secret key, answers exactly like a missing page to anyone without it, and tells browsers never to store it.
  • Your phone's protection. Your library is stored in Qwilted's private storage on your phone, which iOS encrypts using your passcode. The install ID is kept in the iPhone Keychain.

Please be aware:

  • Exported backups aren't encrypted. An exported backup is a plain file with your patches, saves, notes, the text kept from pages and videos, and cover pictures. Anyone who gets the file can read it, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Automatic backups aren't encrypted by Qwilted. They're plain files in Qwilted's storage on your phone, protected by your phone's own encryption.
  • Nothing is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal information, we'll tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Found a security problem? Tell us at support@qwilted.app.
